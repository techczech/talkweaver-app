# Walking the old town

## Before we set off
{id=before-we-set-off}

### The route
{id=w1}

A loop of about two miles, starting and ending in the market square.

### What to wear
{id=w2}

- Flat shoes
- A coat for the hill
- Something bright after dark

### How long it takes
{id=w3}

An hour at an easy pace, with time to stop.

### Meeting point
{id=w4}

By the fountain, ten minutes before the hour.

## The walk
{id=the-walk}

### The market square
{id=w5}

The square was laid out for the cattle fair. The fair has gone; the cobbles stayed.

### The town hall
{id=w6}

Built in stone, with a clock that runs two minutes fast.

### Bakers' Row
{id=w7}

Five ovens once stood in this lane. One still bakes every morning.

### The narrow gate
{id=w8}

The last piece of the old wall. Carts had to wait their turn.

### The river path
{id=w9}

- Willows on one bank
- Allotments on the other
- Herons most mornings

### The mill
{id=w10}

The wheel turned until the floods. The building is now flats.

### The footbridge
{id=w11}

Cast iron, painted green every ten years.

### Church Lane
{id=w12}

The steepest stretch of the walk. Take it slowly.

### The churchyard
{id=w13}

The yew by the porch is older than the church.

### The view from the hill
{id=w14}

On a clear day you can count four church towers.

### The almshouses
{id=w15}

Six small houses round a garden, built for the old and the poor.

### The old school
{id=w16}

One room for every child in the town, until the new school opened.

### The coaching inn
{id=w17}

Horses were changed here on the road to the coast.

### The back lanes
{id=w18}

Narrow, quiet, and the quickest way back to the square.

### Back to the square
{id=w19}

The loop ends where it began. The café on the corner opens at nine.

## After the walk
{id=after-the-walk}

### Questions people ask
{id=w20}

Children are welcome, and dogs on a lead. The town hall has toilets.

### Thank the guides
{id=w21}

Every walk is led by a volunteer.
